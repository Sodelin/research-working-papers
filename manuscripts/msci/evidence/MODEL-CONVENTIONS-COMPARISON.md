# Source conventions and foundational model references

This is a bounded source and attribution check for the manuscript's model description. It is not a proof audit, historical-priority finding, certification of the biological model, or complete literature search.

## The selected source contract

The complete retained contract was read from guide commit `501730afc601aa196ff4c7b84e96105a63199668`, path `recovery/msci/source/f5837e3f1f1604333f5dc6f4c6a23eeca5f95f74.txt`, SHA-256 `dee9fb1a751553e8fdd27d50c81f2cb5456f9d76ad60259f10ddd1d7a1b75d83`. Its sections 1–2 specify the fixed mutation scale, the diploid convention `theta_P = 4 N_P mu`, the **per-current-pair** coalescence rate `2/theta_P`, a single backward B-to-C pulse with independent current-lineage routing, fixed event ages and a root continuing until all sampled lineages coalesce. The pulse corresponds to forward C-to-B introgression; existing C lineages do not move at that pulse. The contract ties `theta_B` to itself across the pulse and separately ties `theta_C` to itself across the pulse; it does not require `theta_B = theta_C`, so `rB` and `rC` may differ.

The contract gives each locus one sampled genealogy, independent JC site evolution conditional on that genealogy, independently uniform root states, and independent repetitions across loci at the same parameters. It adds no missing-data, sequencing-error, ascertainment, alignment-uncertainty, selection or duplication/loss channel. The manuscript's statement that it models no within-locus genealogy switch is an inference from this one-genealogy channel; it is not a claim that all empirical recombination is absent. The contract does not assert that the model fits a dataset.

The retained [55-site theorem](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/df6705e55a830869b8c89e7603edb09cb629d07b/research/2026-10-05-dot-msci-55-site-separation-0508z/THEOREM.md), section 1, writes `r_P=2/theta_P` and states the pair projection on which the later formulas depend. Those retained source assertions and the later scoped hand result are not independently re-proved by this comparison.

The manuscript's population-dependent rate `r_P`, pulse, joins and diploid parameter convention come from the selected contract; Kingman's one-population result alone is not the present source process or a proof of its pair projection.

## Holding times and the population-specific distinction

In the proof of Theorem 1 on printed p. 237, Kingman states the general jump-chain rule `q(s,t)/q(s)` when `q(s)>0`, with termination at a zero-rate state; conditional on the jump chain, the holding times have the appropriate exponential densities and are independent. He then obtains the stronger independence of the **count process** and jump chain in his n-coalescent because the exit rate is the same `k(k-1)/2` for every state with `k` blocks. This source statement supplies useful background; it is not an actual construction or Lean receipt for the manuscript's weighted, scheduled process. If distinct marked events have the same next state, their rates must be aggregated for the state-valued jump probability; retaining event marks is a separate interface.

The equal-block-count simplification does not follow in the present model. Before the pulse, begin with two labelled blocks in each of A, B and C. Merging the B pair and then the C pair leaves population block counts `(2,1,1)` and total merger hazard `rA`; merging the A pair and then the C pair leaves `(1,2,1)` and hazard `rB`. Both have four blocks. When `rA=1` and `rB=2`, these hazards differ, and positive `rC` makes both listed merger sequences available before the positive pulse age. An exact finite bookkeeping control with `rC=3` gave hazards 1 and 2; it is not a stochastic simulation or Lean proof. Thus the construction cannot invoke Kingman's count-process independence solely from the common block count. It must establish the state-dependent holding/mark kernel, the zero-hazard and scheduled-boundary behavior, and conditional innovations or an equivalent Markov construction for this source.
