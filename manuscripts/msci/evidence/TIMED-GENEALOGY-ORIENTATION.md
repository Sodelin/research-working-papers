The distinction between present blocks and ancestral history matters. Consider two abstract six-label histories with all leaves at age zero. In the first, merge labels 0 and 1 at age 1, add label 2 at age 2, then add 3, 4 and 5 at ages 3, 4 and 5. In the second, first merge 0 and 2, then add 1, followed by the same additions at ages 3, 4 and 5. Both end with the one block `{0,1,2,3,4,5}` in the same population, but the age of the most recent common ancestor of 0 and 1 is 1 in the first history and 2 in the second. This is an abstract counterexample to recovering labelled merger history from final block/population data alone. It does **not** assert that either complete history has positive probability under the selected source programme or its fixed sample locations.

## A permitted child swap

For the explicit pruning recursion (internal supporting file not included: `JCFinitePruningDraft.lean`), write `L_w(T,a)` for the likelihood at ancestral state `a`. At a branch it is a product of two finite sums, one for each child. Swapping the children **together with their own edge attenuations** merely reverses those two factors. Induction extends this equality to arbitrary nested child swaps, keeping every tip label and every child/attenuation pairing fixed. Uniform-root averaging then gives equal conditional site masses (internal supporting file not included: `JCPanelMassDraft.lean`), and their two-site product agrees when both sites use the same reoriented tree. The algebra requires no claim that the tree is an actual genealogy or even that its weights form valid stochastic tables.

The edge pairing cannot be dropped. At root state 0, take a left tip labelled 0 observed as state 0, a right tip labelled 1 observed as state 1, and left/right attenuations `3/4` and `1/4`. The two factors give conditional likelihood `39/256`. Swapping just the children while leaving those attenuations in their old positions gives `7/256`; these values follow directly from the displayed JC transition table. This is a root-conditional arithmetic illustration, not a comparison of the two complete source laws. Both chosen attenuations lie strictly between 0 and 1.

## Equal-age resolutions require more than child swaps

Take three distinct sampled labels `0,1,2` and a positive age `τ`. One binary genealogy first joins `0,1` at `τ` and then joins `2` at that same age; another first joins `1,2` at `τ` and then joins `0`. Both give age `τ` to all three pairwise most recent common ancestors. They are not related by child swaps alone: the first contains the labelled clade `{0,1}`, while the second contains `{1,2}`. Attach labels `3,4,5` above both in the same later order to obtain two six-label examples with the same complete pair-age matrix. This is an abstract tie example, not a claim that the actual source produces such a tie with positive probability.

The two resolutions nevertheless have the same conditional JC likelihood. Their internal edge between equal-age mergers has attenuation `1`, so its transition table is the identity. For an ancestral nucleotide `a`, each resolution's pruning expression reduces to the same three child-likelihood factors at `a`, one per attached subtree. Uniform-root averaging and the product of the two site masses preserve that equality. The zero-edge tree candidate (internal supporting file not included: `JCTreeZeroEdgeEquivDraft.lean`) attempts this rotation algebra and closes it under existing child orientations, nesting, symmetry and transitivity. It does not prove that all trees with the same pair-age matrix are related by those moves, or that the source and physical trees are related at all; it and its imports remain uncompiled and unreviewed.

This zero-length **genealogy** edge is distinct from the zero-duration donor edge in the population network. The example shows that pair-age equality alone cannot imply the stronger `JCTreeOrientation` relation when equal merger ages are admitted. A same-record proof that retains the actual ordered merger events could still establish that stronger relation. Alternatively, a weaker observation-preserving relation would need an actual source-to-physical construction and proof, including any boundary-tie or no-tie facts used. No conclusion about an actual tie probability, the full physical process or Lean verification follows from this example.

$$
 M(i,j)\ge0,\qquad A_t(i)=A_t(j)\iff M(i,j)\le t,
$$

where `M` and the ancestor path `A` both come from that **same** complete source record. Assume this whole statement for one record. Because `M(j,i)` is nonnegative, it is an allowed time. At that time the two ancestors agree in the `j,i` order, hence also in the `i,j` order; the threshold gives `M(i,j) ≤ M(j,i)`. Interchanging the labels gives equality of the two entries. The same threshold shows that once a pair has joined at `t`, it remains joined at every later `u ≥ t`.

If the pair is known to be separate at **every** nonnegative time strictly before `τ` and joined at `τ`, its matrix entry is exactly `τ`: joining at `τ` gives `M(i,j) ≤ τ`; if `M(i,j) < τ`, the threshold at the allowed time `M(i,j)` contradicts the before-`τ` separation. Symmetry supplies the reversed entry as well. This argument includes `τ=0` without assuming an earlier time exists.

### A local first-joining reduction

The source definitions also support the following hand argument, conditional on the stated legal-trace and decoder interfaces; it is not yet a Lean theorem. Take one legal, active-prefix, nondecreasing local trace and a merger row actually emitted by its inclusive cut at `v`. Let the row's time be `τ`, its before-code be `s`, and its actual source choice merge distinct live owners `a` and `b`, retaining `a`. Suppose original labels `i` and `j` have owners `a` and `b` in `s`, respectively. For the decoded endpoint of this **same** trace, `i` and `j` have distinct owners at every local cut `t < τ` and the same owner at every cut `t ≥ τ`.

To see this, associate the emitted row with the slot that produced it. Since that slot is active, every earlier slot is active with time at most `τ`; its recorded before-code is the result after those earlier included slots. At a cut `t < τ`, only a prefix of those earlier slots can be included. Every legal merger maps equal owners to equal owners, so equality at that earlier cut would persist through the remaining earlier slots and contradict the different owners `a` and `b` in `s`. At a cut `t ≥ τ`, all earlier slots and this slot are included; the merger sends both labels to `a`, and later included legal mergers preserve their equality. The argument allows earlier or later slots tied at `τ`: an inclusive cut takes the whole tied group in its stored order, and no subsequent merger splits a joined pair.

## The missing coupling

A candidate invariant can be stated on one **complete** retained record `z`. Let `M(z)` be its original final pair-age matrix. For every current block `b`, use source validity to associate `b` with its unique live source genealogy `g_b`, and compare it with the proposed scheduled forest `F` only on blocks actually present:

$$
 F.\operatorname{age}(b)=\operatorname{sourceGenealogyNodeAge}(M(z),g_b),\qquad
 \operatorname{JCTreeOrientation}\bigl(F.\operatorname{tree}(b),
             \operatorname{sourceGenealogyJCTree}(M(z),g_b)\bigr).
$$

This is a **proposed invariant**, not a proved predicate or an established existence statement for `F`. It uses the completed record's final matrix to verify a whole path retrospectively; that does not permit using future information to choose an earlier physical event. For a merger at age `τ` of current blocks `u,v`, the needed source fact is that the final matrix entry across their original leaves is `τ`, including both orders when the source children are reversed. Earlier within-block entries must remain the earlier event ages. Under those facts and the two child induction hypotheses, the source graft and `jcForestMerge` use the same age differences, so their branches are related by `keep` or `swap`.
