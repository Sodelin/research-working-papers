The paper also describes tracing a lineage through a hybrid node, the `4Nμ` population convention, one shared genealogy within a locus and independent genealogies among loci under fixed model parameters. ## Independent, common and correlated routing

John Fogg, Elizabeth S. Allman and Cécile Ané, “[PhyloCoalSimulations: A Simulator for Network Multispecies Coalescent Models, Including a New Extension for the Inheritance of Gene Flow](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/72/5/1171/7187011),” *Systematic Biology* 72(5), 1171–1179 (2023), distinguishes independent inheritance across lineages at a reticulation from common inheritance and develops a correlated alternative. The indexed source text states that the latter can interpolate between inheritance assumptions. This manuscript fixes independent choices for the **current** B blocks. It does not treat independence across distinct blocks as independence among descendants already combined in one block.

For two distinct B blocks present at the pulse, the stated independent-Bernoulli rule gives `g²` for both moving, `(1-g)²` for both staying, and `2g(1-g)` for a split. A single shared Bernoulli choice with the same marginal `g` would give `g`, `1-g` and `0`, respectively. These are elementary conditional routing probabilities, not observed sequence probabilities, a simulation result, or a proof about another model's identifiability. The contrast explains why one cannot silently substitute a common coin in the manuscript's BB formula. The original source theorem does not assess whether independent or correlated routing fits a biological dataset.

On 1 November 2026 UTC, I also read the authors' [PhyloCoalSimulations documentation on correlated inheritance](https://juliaphylo.github.io/PhyloCoalSimulations.jl/dev/man/correlated_inheritance/). It describes a particular positive-correlation model: for two still-distinct lineages at a hybrid node, the second copies the first lineage's chosen parent with probability `ρ`, and otherwise chooses according to the same parental probabilities. Here `ρ` is the documentation's inheritance-correlation parameter, not one of this manuscript's population merger rates. The documentation page is a mutable development edition, reported as generated on 22 June 2026; no source-code version was selected and the simulator was not run.

Under that stated two-lineage rule, if the marginal probability of routing to C is `g`, the probability that the second also moves given that the first moved is `ρ+(1-ρ)g`. Elementary conditioning gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{pulse outcome} & \text{probability}\\\hline
\text{both move to C} & g^2+\rho g(1-g)\\
\text{both stay in B} & (1-g)^2+\rho g(1-g)\\
\text{one moves, one stays} & 2(1-\rho)g(1-g).
\end{array}
$$

For `ρ=0` these are the manuscript's independent-block weights; for `ρ=1` they are the common-parent weights. At an interior `g`, a positive `ρ` changes the two-block weights. This is a comparison with one specified alternative, conditional on both blocks being distinct at the pulse. It does not establish the full six-lineage joint law, fit a biological dataset, or show that the manuscript's formal source implements correlated inheritance.

The full source-to-source parameter and probability-law mapping, independent scientific review and the Lean-checked stochastic/observation bridge remain open. This note does not turn a related implementation, an alternative simulator, or a bounded source reading into a proof or a usable locus-count recommendation.
