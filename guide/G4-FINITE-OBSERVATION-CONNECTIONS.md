Its useful outputs are finite experiment certificates in specified classes, exact source-to-observation identities, and counterchecks showing why several proposed shortcuts do not answer the remaining question. This Codex/OpenAI synthesis preserves the original contributors' attribution and historical AI review status. It is not a new proof or independent human review.

## The same source must answer every experiment

The original target is a finite strictly positive rooted-LSA, outer-labelled planar, cut-child binary source, with original taxa and parallel arc occurrences retained. Rivals have unknown finite size. Each source uses one physical tuple across all allocations and modes, with its original ties. These modes represent different observation contracts. Fitting their rows with different sources or parameters does not meet the original problem.

## The remaining question has an exact acceptance test

An accepted entire-law transfer says equality of **all** natural I responses implies equality of all natural C responses. That is not finite extraction. For each fixed target T, the remaining finite-I bridge asks for a finite legal I panel J_T whose exact agreement forces a fixed finite COMMON certificate C_T against every admitted finite rival. Combined with the existing BOTH certificate, this would give finite I-only determination.

A negative result instead needs **one fixed strict target**, and for every finite permitted I panel an actual finite strict rival matching that whole panel exactly while differing later. Changing the target, matching only selected diagonals, approximate convergence or producing a signed/formal kernel does not satisfy that test. This is a project-posed open question in the inspected records, not an identified solution or newly established status for a published external problem.

The reviewed abstract quadrature countermodel shows why entire-I transfer, finite BOTH determination and bounded complexity of full-I fibres are insufficient **as generic logical premises**. In its mixture grammar, Gaussian quadrature produces exact finite I-pattern matches with a different C value. The grammar is not an admitted coalescent network. The physical weak-cell construction instead yields exact low-copy agreement and convergent higher-copy laws, with separated COMMON responses. Its higher-copy agreement is approximate. Neither construction supplies the original negative alternative.
