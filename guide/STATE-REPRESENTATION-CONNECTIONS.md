# Three useful results and the bridges they still need

These connections concern retained source arguments already selected in the theorem guide. They do not introduce a new proof, implementation, compiler run, current literature survey or human review. The common practical question is precise: which information must a representation retain for the intended prediction or decision?

The first connection is useful for choosing a certificate representation: discarding state can lose a conserved potential. It is an instance of established potential reweighting, not a claim to invent that method. The standard potential and cycle-mean background includes [Karp’s work](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1977/Archive/ERL-m-77-47.pdf). Its static optimum one is a limitation of the specified certificate family, not an existing speed-one trajectory. The two-cell period-two example attains horizontal speed zero. The source's historical Lean receipts and finite controls have their recorded scope; this note performs no fresh proof-closure audit.

The second connects the retained infinite-state formal statements to established finite-state verification. Product graphs, strongly connected components and Büchi emptiness are prior methods; see [Sistla and Yannakakis](https://research.ibm.com/publications/memory-efficient-algorithms-for-the-verification-of-temporal-properties) and [Tarjan](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0201010?journalCode=smjcat). The missing contribution is a faithful finite scientific representation, rather than another generic graph algorithm. Finite branching is essential: a countably branching root with one finite branch of every length can survive every finite pruning round without an infinite path. [Alexander's published periodic unavoidability theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.0186v2) has different population hypotheses; this finite-graph receiver does not contradict it or solve the general classification.

The third exposes a quantifier and measurement-schedule barrier. The supplied one-attempt witness uses the balanced 12³ ideal chain and every-attempt descriptors; the sampled test fixes the initializer, temperature, equilibration and many-attempt recording block. Paths may leave and return to an observation fibre inside a block. The retained sampled test has positive denominators and an explicit determinant for two histories, but its nonvanishing remains open. Its nonvanishing would distinguish those histories, not establish infinite memory order or exclude a latent predictive state. The positive finite-history separation can decrease with history length and does not rule out useful approximation.

These are three distinct further questions. None is labelled a currently open named conjecture without an independent current-source review. A belief state or state-aware quotient can provide an exact finite-model representation in suitable settings, but dimension alone supplies no precision, efficiency or physical-calibration guarantee. The already-published controlled-state map provides an exact finite adaptive-history contract; adapters to these three models have not been established.
