## What the construction actually does

With original internal inheritance weights fixed strictly positive, resolve parent choices into selectors σ with weights wσ. Give every edge entering a selected internal sink the same matrix A, and other internal edges a reset with parameter t∈(0,1). The proof's graph argument supplies at least one positive-weight path of length at least two. Thus

`c(t)=Σσ wσ t^(Lσ−1)`

is below one and approaches one. Choosing a finite t sufficiently close to one, then compensating A, realizes an intended port table exactly without setting an inheritance probability to zero or an edge to identity. One assignment works across all selectors; it is not a separately fitted mixture component for each displayed tree.

For the uniform-root Fourier domains, with U the uniform reset and target strictly positive matrix M, the proof sets `A=U+(M−U)/c` after explicit positivity/eigenvalue guards. The full port image is `(1/4)M` for that positive-entry subfamily, even though individual admitted original edge matrices may have zero entries. The argument does not split an arbitrary zero-entry matrix into two admitted factors.

For the full open equivariant domain, it writes the target joint table as `Dμ M` and uses a μ-stationary reversible reset. The compensation is ordered so no unjustified commutation with arbitrary A is needed. The root law may change to μ; a prescribed root law is outside that theorem. For strict continuous-time K2P, the condition `y>x²` and the corresponding rate inversion ensure both sides stay in the same strict rate domain.

The unchanged exterior is one joint stochastic kernel Q(z|i,j). Matching the whole port table gives the leaf law by summing `P_ab(i,j) Q(z|i,j)`. Reconverging exterior paths do not require independent descendant marginals. This is a reusable exact replacement method under the stated graph, parameter and context contracts.

## The second external question requires another graph adapter

Brits and coauthors' v3 Section 5.1 defines a 2-sub-blob through two boundary **vertices** and states a root-trapping inclusion/closure result, leaving exact equality open. The retained direct theorem uses two boundary **edge occurrences** of a maximal rooted region. Two vertices do not imply two edge occurrences. [Primary definition and Lemma 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12919v3#S5.SS1).

The retained [definition reconciliation](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/27b143c73a4ac272b560b2974d83ff96df738531/research/2026-10-22-dot-phylogeny-root-blob/SUBBLOB-DEFINITION-RECONCILIATION.md) records a region with two boundary vertices and three crossing occurrences. This diagnoses a definition/operation compatibility issue; it is not a counterexample to an already well-defined published image equality. Internal bridgelessness and hybrid-closedness in a stronger cited convention cannot be imported silently into the weaker printed contract. A universal compatible rooting/lift, suppression interpretation and parameter transport remain unsupplied in the inspected packet.

A conditional corrected rooted-region adapter is retained, but it does not remove that universal obligation. Likewise, deleting degree-two ports or identifying parallel edges needs an actual model-preserving map; an unrestricted collapsed-edge parameterization can change the image.
