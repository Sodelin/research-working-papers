## Legal moment data need not have finite rank

A retained ordinary-target anchor construction uses disjoint copy blocks on one completed genealogy, with a fixed anchor panel. Their patterns are exchangeable but not new independent loci. Its directing probability is Beta(1,2), with moments

`E[P^k]=2/[(k+1)(k+2)]`.

Every finite moment Hankel block is positive definite, so ranks grow without bound. Even this finite ordinary source therefore does not furnish a finite-dimensional **linear copy-append realization for that encoding**. A nonlinear state, another observation encoding or a target-specific certificate remains possible.

The weighted-automata finite-Hankel-rank characterization is classical; Balle and Mohri attribute it to Carlyle–Paz and Fliess. It supplies a relevant applicability test, not a finite-rank premise for the anchor law above. Likewise, Curto–Fialkow's flat-extension theorem needs a positive semidefinite, rank-preserving moment extension. A finite atomic representation of truncated moments is not automatically the full directing law or an admitted physical network. [Primary weighted-automata statement](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/700fdb2ba62d4554dc268c65add4b16e-Paper.pdf), [primary flat-extension abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507067v1).

## A latent motif is not an extra legal observation

For private independent words, the auxiliary route graphon's clique densities equal no-merger probabilities. Other motif densities need not equal permitted forest events. Transitive coalescent mergers already separate a path-motif value from the corresponding pair-separation event in an ordinary population.

The reviewed hidden-C4 result is stronger than absence of one rational formula: for each fixed finite cap, actual strict positive source words can share the entire capped forest kernel while having different latent C4 values. The sources can vary with the cap. This prevents any uniform function of that capped interface from recovering that statistic, but it is not one fixed target with exact rivals at every cap.

A separate reviewed three-bigon construction matches one ordinary target through four roots and differs at a later legal topology observation. Its root is specified by an exact interval fixed-point certificate; approximate center digits are not the source parameter. This fixed-target **one-cap** example still does not supply the negative master alternative for every cap. The auxiliary route graphon can also forget chronology: two strict words with the same static route representation have different complete four-root forest probabilities after ordinary passages are reordered. Thus a theorem about the static kernel does not automatically preserve the chronological source action or provide an authorized completed-topology readout.

Shapira and Tyomkyn already prove that all clique densities do not force quasirandomness in their graphon setting. Their graphons are not automatically admitted finite coalescent words. Step-kernel forcing results likewise assume actual graph densities; the physical experiment needs a legal decoder for them. Neither comparison establishes a solution or counterexample to a graphon-forcing open problem.
