# Sharp phase windows as a recognizability interface

The [retained public hand-reading edition](https://github.com/Sodelin/research-working-papers/blob/98e38d4ce6384cb5051b42ef30f758e68dcda688/manuscripts/sequence/Pattern-Hierarchies-and-Selected-Digit-Proofs.md) gives an exact one-sided phase threshold for τₙ(0)=0ⁿ1ⁿ, τₙ(1)=1ⁿ0ⁿ, uₙ=τₙ^ω(0), q=2n. With n,k≥1 known, the least factor length at which every factor determines its occurrence residue modulo q^k is Dₙ(k)=q^(k−1)(4n²−n)+1. An ambiguous factor at the preceding length establishes sharpness. This identifies a construction grid relative to a window, not absolute position or a unique occurrence.

This connects to [Durand–Leroy's general computable recognizability bounds](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL20/Leroy/leroy4.pdf), with distinct window conventions. It is a sharp special-family calculation, not an established solution of a named general open problem or a new general recognizability algorithm. Attribution is dot (OpenAI), with recorded AI hand acceptance, no Lean/human review and some missing original hashed component review/checker receipts. The edition's byte identity is pinned in [the selected-digit map](SELECTED-DIGIT-CONNECTIONS.md).

What this does: gives an exact finite amount of noiseless local information sufficient to locate the hidden substitution grid in this family. It supports a concrete next question about phase-label separation under specified errors. Classical Hamming-ball separation gives a criterion for substitution errors once complete phase-labelled factor sets are supplied; neither that elementary criterion nor a few finite examples prove a general noisy-window formula. Noise admission and specialist/priority review remain distinct work. No empirical sequence model or general alphabet extension is claimed.
