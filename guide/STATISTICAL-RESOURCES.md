## Heavy/light testing inside a physical Gibbs class

The [component32 packet](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/27b143c73a4ac272b560b2974d83ff96df738531/research/2026-10-16-g6-gibbs-certification/latent-light-fraction-32/README.md) explicitly credits Canonne–Diakonikolas–Kane–Stewart's [Testing Bayesian Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v65/canonne17a.html). Its stated lower bound is for two unknown Gibbs states at fixed β>0, locality k≥5, 0<ε≤1/4096 and sufficiently large physical N, with coefficientwise-bounded terms but unrestricted interaction degree. Put a=min((2k+2)/3,(4k−1)/5). It reports Ω(N^a/(log N)^(a/(k−1))) total copies even allowing collective measurements and adaptive source choice. All ancillary spins count; the hard subclass is diagonal. This is not a bounded-degree theorem, an optimal ε/β rate, or a matching full-class solution.

The retained author/reviewer argument uses one latent light fraction per label, fixed across sources/copies, and all-count likelihood rather than just a two-copy calculation. Complemented conditional means lie outside an earlier fixed alphabet; prior correlation by itself does not defeat a pointwise tester. The packet has distinct AI statistical and physical/reduction reviews, finite controls and no human/Lean/priority certificate. Specialist assessment of the resource-preserving construction is the useful next step.

## A restricted tester using the optimal marginal theorem

The [unequal-marginal alphabet ceiling](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/27b143c73a4ac272b560b2974d83ff96df738531/research/2026-10-16-g6-gibbs-certification/random-label-masses-29/ceilings/unequal-marginals-29c/README.md) imports Chan–Diakonikolas–Valiant–Valiant's [optimal discrete closeness theorem](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pvaliant/closeness.pdf), not a new general marginal tester. A pooled pilot and paired conditional-bit test handle a specified known alphabet with arbitrary unequal or zero label masses. The complete hand derivation and separate AI review retain the imported absolute constant, pre-query caps, all discarded copies and source-size/retention conditions.

Crucially, row extraction alone does not transfer a physical far promise. One common source-independent CPTP encoding and explicit approximation errors imply the required row-distance lower bound. Unknown conditional means, dependent rows or a different encoding require new arguments. This restricts particular lower-bound constructions and supplies a valid restricted-family tester; it does not upper-bound all local Gibbs testing. A cleaner existing statistical theorem or broader admitted alphabet is a concrete possible follow-on, not a completed claim.

## Finite exact certificates for comparison of experiments

The [compact deficiency certificate](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/f87a6c50029c1ae13f0340818b3bf358b1bf3de3/research/2026-09-30-general-transfer-closure/compact-certificate.md) connects classical Le Cam decision comparison to a returned simulator. For supplied finite alphabets, compact parameter cover, valid uniform TV moduli and simultaneous grid-law errors, rational primal/dual witnesses bound deficiency and certify the same returned kernel over the full parameter class. A positive lower margin yields a finite-prior decision disadvantage. These are model/access premises, not facts inferred by the checker.

Its [Python prototype](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/f87a6c50029c1ae13f0340818b3bf358b1bf3de3/research/2026-09-30-general-transfer-closure/compact_certificate.py) separates proposed optimization from exact rational checking. Historical AI reviews and execution evidence are not a fresh run, Lean proof or general infinite-alphabet solver. Refinement can decide separated thresholds but does not promise termination at equality/zero. [Reiß's equivalence-of-experiments work](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607342) is a relevant specialist interface, not a theorem imported into this prototype or a solved external open problem.
