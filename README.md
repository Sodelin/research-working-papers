This collection makes a selection of AI-assisted mathematical research available for discussion, correction and collaboration. Each reading edition states its model, sources and current checking status. **Sodelin organizes and shares the project**; the underlying proof sources and AI contributions are credited in the individual papers. A final scientific author list has not been established.

These are working papers, not journal articles or claims of established priority. Some components have reported formal checks; those reports apply only to the declarations and assumptions identified in the papers. None of the papers claims independent human peer review or a validation on biological data. Corrections and relevant prior work are welcome through this repository's issue tracker.

## Papers

1. [Two-site identification in a fixed three-taxon pulse model](manuscripts/msci/MANUSCRIPT.md). A proposed identifiability result for a specified coalescent-with-introgression model. The [short overview](manuscripts/msci/evidence/RESEARCH-OVERVIEW-DRAFT.md) introduces the assumptions and open questions. Reported Lean checking covers the analytic formulas, not the full process-to-observation connection. The [exact source edition](source-editions/msci-052d3ee/README.md) is also preserved for comparison.
2. [Distinguishing an impossible event from a rare event](manuscripts/calibration/MANUSCRIPT.md). Sharp testing errors in a simple fixed-rate independent-trial model, with a separately reported formal component. The probability calculation is elementary; no novelty claim is made.
3. [Two observations do not make hidden probabilities deterministic](manuscripts/product-coin/MANUSCRIPT.md). An abstract product-Bernoulli example distinguishing constraints on observations from constraints on latent factors.
4. [Two coordinates for exchangeable three-lineage forests](manuscripts/cap-three/MANUSCRIPT.md). An abstract forest-law calculation and a four-label illustration under stated projectivity and serial-kernel assumptions.
5. [How a fixed model's observable law changes with its parameters](manuscripts/g6-fixed-cell/MANUSCRIPT.md). A standard polynomial bound and a scoped application to one fixed graph and cell; this is not the full G6 programme.
6. [Thue–Morse first occurrences, matching heights and juggling](manuscripts/sequence/Thue-Morse-Hand-Proofs.md). A complete preserved hand edition; the linked formal package covers specific first-occurrence statements, while the later consequences are hand results.
7. [Pattern hierarchies and selected-digit words](manuscripts/sequence/Pattern-Hierarchies-and-Selected-Digit-Proofs.md). Preserved hand proofs and prior-work comparisons for two distinct word families, including an explicit Cloitre correction. No Lean claim is made for this edition.

## Connections to research questions

New source-based reading guides address [finite observation transfers](guide/FINITE-OBSERVATION-TRANSFERS.md), [demographic exact-law targets](guide/DEMOGRAPHIC-LAWS.md), and [cellular measurement contracts](guide/CELLULAR-MEASUREMENTS.md). Each states its observation model, prior work and independent-review gaps.

Four source-based guides distinguish the assumptions behind [RNA class inference](guide/RNA-CLASS-INFERENCE.md), [reaction observations](guide/REACTION-OBSERVATIONS.md), [lineage inference](guide/LINEAGE-INFERENCE.md), and [finite sampling and ancestry](guide/FINITE-SAMPLING.md). They link usable conditional results to specified outside questions and retain the missing model bridges, earlier work and review limits.

[Root-blob exact-image questions](guide/ROOT-BLOB-QUESTIONS.md) connects a
retained-port proof to two version-specific phylogenetic questions, with the
transition-model and graph-operation gaps stated.

[Experimental design connections](guide/EXPERIMENTAL-DESIGN-CONNECTIONS.md)
sets out the exact-law G7 design result, static forcing costs and a separate
quartet-support oracle, with their observation and resource assumptions.

[G6 robust certification](guide/G6-ROBUST-CERTIFICATION-CONNECTIONS.md)
explains honest finite conclusions, abstention and the observation and
implementation requirements that remain.

[G3 recognition methods](guide/G3-RECOGNITION-CONNECTIONS.md) and
[G4 finite observations](guide/G4-FINITE-OBSERVATION-CONNECTIONS.md) set out
usable scoped results and the total-decision and finite-observation bridges
that remain open. Their contract tables preserve inconclusive outcomes.

[Phylogenetic observation connections](guide/PHYLOGENETIC-OBSERVATION-CONNECTIONS.md)
reconciles the selected core, sampling, fixed-family MSci and NANUQ results
with the scope of their underlying sources and reported formal checks.

[Sequence recovery and alignment](guide/SEQUENCE-ALIGNMENT-CONNECTIONS.md),
[statistical decision contracts](guide/STATISTICAL-DECISION-CONTRACTS.md) and
[physics task contracts](guide/PHYSICS-TASK-CONTRACTS.md) distinguish the
existing results from missing proofs, model bridges and external prior work.

[State, ancestry and memory](guide/STATE-REPRESENTATION-CONNECTIONS.md)
explains three existing results on certificates, finite-state liveness and
materials descriptors, with the separate scientific bridges still needed.

[Moments, noisy imaging and compatible sets](guide/MOMENTS-IMAGING-CONNECTIONS.md)
connects an exact sparse-moment component, a Fourier–Poisson risk baseline and
a conditional diffraction certificate to the questions and missing inputs for
each. The companion [Fourier reading copy](guide/sources/FOURIER-POISSON-READING.md)
preserves the mathematical source while omitting unverified navigation links.

[What the work connects to](guide/CONNECTIONS.md) is a source-linked selection
from the research-connections owner's guide. It describes nine mathematical
or scientific questions, the contributions claimed in the existing sources,
and the precise assumptions or gaps that remain. It complements the papers
above and does not treat overlapping records as separate discoveries.

[Existing public problem records](guide/PUBLIC-RECORDS.md) links a further
selection of the project's material already listed at VibeMathed, with concise
scope notes and links to each site's source and verification details.

The [working programme reading guide](guide/programme/README.md) brings
together twelve existing research chapters and a dated source-linked catalogue.
It distinguishes open questions, hand arguments, reported formal components
and missing source references; its entries are not counts of new discoveries.
